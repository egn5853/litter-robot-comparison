# litter robot: how to choose between EVO, 4, 5 and 5 Pro without paying for features your home can't use

Most people searching for a Litter-Robot are stuck on the same two questions: is the thing actually worth $600–$900, and if it is, which model. The brand's own site doesn't make the second question easy, because right now Whisker sells four automatic litter boxes at four different prices, and the spec differences are buried in a comparison chart with rows like "headroom" and "WasteID."

So here's the short version before the detail. If you have one cat in a small apartment, the EVO is the sensible entry point. If you have two or three cats and want the version with the fewest known headaches, the Litter-Robot 4 is still the model most reviewers default to. If you have four or five cats, or you want the newest hardware, the 5 is the current mainstream pick. The 5 Pro is the one to think hardest about, because you're paying roughly $100 more for cameras and health features that reviewers have had real trouble with.

## What you're buying, and what doesn't change

All four robots work the same way. Your cat goes in, the sensors register the visit, and a minute or so after they leave, the globe rotates and sifts: clumps and solids drop into a sealed drawer underneath, clean litter falls back into the drum. That's the whole trick, and it's the same trick Whisker has been refining since the first Litter-Robot shipped in 2000.

What disappears is scooping. What stays is everything else: someone still has to empty the drawer (roughly once a week with one cat, more often with several), replace carbon filters, and wash the globe every so often. Whisker's own guidance is spot cleaning every week or two and a deep clean once a season; long-term owners are blunter about it, with one reviewer at the Chicago Tribune who has run an LR4 since 2022 saying hers works best with a monthly deep clean and that the job is genuinely awkward in an apartment without a hose.

Two practical things worth knowing before you spend anything. The robot cycles without an internet connection, so a Wi-Fi dropout doesn't strand a dirty globe. And it needs a power outlet where it sits, because nothing about the cycle is manual.

> Litter compatibility is narrower than most product pages make it sound. Whisker's own product listings specify clumping mineral (clay) litter, note that silica or crystal litter is usable only if the granules pass the sieve, and list non-clumping litter, paper pellets and wood pellets as incompatible. If you're loyal to pellets, you're changing litter brands as well as litter boxes.

There's also a minimum cat weight of about 3 lbs for automatic mode. Kittens below that can use it powered off as a regular box, then you run a cycle by hand until they're heavy enough, which is the acclimation path Whisker itself describes.

### Litter-Robot EVO: the small-space and first-timer pick

The EVO is the most compact robot Whisker has made, and it's the only one that plausibly fits the floor space your old pan box occupied. The trade-offs are concrete rather than vague: two cats maximum, a smaller waste drawer, no litter-level sensor (so the app won't nag you when litter runs low), no night light, and no LitterHopper support. Mashable's reviewer, who ran one for months in an apartment, found the cycle almost inaudibly quiet and reliable in normal operation, but noted the scale and sensors can be temperamental about litter volume, and that the drawer still smells when you're kneeling next to it even after the liner is swapped. At $599 it's the cheapest way in, and it's the model that gets you the automatic cycle without the health-data pitch.

### Litter-Robot 4: the safe middle

The 4 is the generation that made the category mainstream, and four years on it's still what several reviewers reach for. Good Housekeeping's tester, who has lived with both the 4 and the 5 Pro, still names the $700 Litter-Robot 4 her overall pick and points out that it does the same four core jobs as the newer model for a few hundred dollars less. It supports up to four cats, tracks weight and visits in the free Whisker app, senses litter level, and works with the LitterHopper auto-refill accessory. Its weaknesses are well documented: a soft rubber liner that eventually tears (replacement is straightforward), an LED panel that's less intuitive than the new colour screens, and a smaller drawer than the 5-series.

### Litter-Robot 5: the current default for busy multi-cat homes

The 5 keeps the same sifting mechanism but adds WasteID, which distinguishes urine from feces and changes cycle timing accordingly, a bigger drawer, quieter operation, and a colour LCD that tells you what the robot is doing instead of making you decode blinking lights. It also covers cats up to 30 lbs and up to five cats, and it's LitterHopper compatible. In theory the waste-type data is the most useful health feature Whisker has shipped, because litter box frequency and urine volume are exactly what vets ask about.

### Litter-Robot 5 Pro: buy it for the cameras, not the AI

The 5 Pro adds two night-vision cameras and AI-driven cat identification, which matters if you have two cats of similar size and want to know which one is visiting. It also works with the $29 Whisker PetTag RFID tag.

Here's where it gets uncomfortable. The Verge spent six months with the 5 Pro and the EVO and came away saying it wouldn't pay the premium: its WasteID repeatedly misread waste types, and the cameras confused a large grey tabby with a small tuxedo cat, eventually merging them into two identical cats. That reviewer also found that only the front-facing camera records video; the interior camera is live view only, which contradicts what the ad imagery implies. BGR had motor faults and phantom "extended scale activity" warnings roughly weekly, plus a rough app setup. ZDNET, testing with two cats, found the AI still learning after two and a half months, and noted Whisker was preparing a further algorithm update.

So: the bigger drawer, brighter screen and quieter cycle are genuine. The smart features are the part to treat as experimental.

## The running costs nobody puts in the product photo

The robot doesn't remove spending, it moves it. Budget for waste drawer liners, carbon filters, OdorTrap packs if you use them, and litter. Whisker's own product pages are careful to avoid claiming litter savings, and a Japanese-language buying guide on the brand's own store puts it plainly: litter consumption doesn't drop, because you're still topping up the level and removing clumps, just with a machine doing the removal.

WhiskerCare extended coverage is listed at $129 for three years, with the first year already included at purchase. Whisker+, the optional subscription that unlocks daily recaps, time-of-day trends, up to two years of history and video storage on camera models, runs $7.99 a month, or roughly $80 a year. There's a one-month free trial. The free app already covers weight-based cat IDs, drawer alerts, litter level and yesterday's visit duration, so Whisker+ is mainly a multi-cat or health-tracking purchase.

One more cost people miss: getting a robot that isn't yours. Amazon's Canada listing for the Litter-Robot 4 sits at 4.1 stars across roughly 199 ratings, and the one-star reviews are the sort of thing to read before you buy — sensor errors, a "drawer full" warning on an empty drawer, and a calibration process the reviewer described as painful. Not universal problems, but the failure modes are real.

## Who should not buy one

Honest disqualifiers, since none of them are in the marketing:

- You use non-clumping litter. Paper pellets, wood pellets and system-toilet litter won't sift. You'd be switching litter at the same time as litter boxes.
- Your cat is under about 3 lbs. Automatic mode won't detect them reliably.
- There's no outlet where the box goes. The cycle needs power, always.
- You have a genuinely tight spot. The 5 and 5 Pro are 29.9" tall with a 22" x 27" footprint, and the robot can't sit flush against a wall because the globe rotates. Measure before ordering, not after.
- You want out of cleaning entirely. Filters, liners, wipes and periodic deep cleans remain your job.

The acceptance question has no answer until you try it. Whisker says 95% of cats will eventually use an automatic box; a Vetstreet tester found her smaller cat adapted within a day while her larger, enclosure-averse male hadn't touched the EVO after two weeks. That uncertainty is the reason the 90-day trial matters, though a US reviewer's account notes you cover return shipping.

## Quick answers to the questions that come up most

**Does the robot need Wi-Fi to work?** No. It cycles when it loses connection. Wi-Fi only feeds the app.

**How often do you empty it?** Roughly weekly with one cat on the Litter-Robot 4 bundles, which Whisker lists as up to eight days for a single-cat home. The 5-series drawers are bigger, and more cats means more often.

**Is the 5 Pro worth the extra $100 over the 5?** Only if you specifically want per-cat identification in a multi-cat home and can live with features that are still maturing. Reviewers who tested it longest were the least convinced.

**Can you use HSA/FSA funds?** Whisker sells an HSA/FSA bundle and covers the $30 letter-of-medical-necessity fee, with orders typically processed within 24 hours. You select the provider at checkout and complete the letter as part of the order.

**Which one would most people be happy with?** The Litter-Robot 4, at $699, does scoop-free cycling, weight tracking, litter-level sensing and multi-cat support, and it's the model with years of review history behind it. The 5 makes sense if you want the larger drawer, taller interior for big cats, and five-cat capacity. ## The bottom line

A Litter-Robot is a $599–$899 appliance plus maybe $100 a year in consumables and, if you want the deeper data, $7.99 a month. It eliminates scooping and cuts odour by shortening how long waste sits in the box. It does not eliminate cat-care chores, litter costs, or cleaning.

Pick the model by your household, not by the feature list. Two cats in an apartment: EVO. Two to four cats and you want the proven version: Litter-Robot 4. Four or five cats, or big cats up to 30 lbs: Litter-Robot 5. Two cats that look alike and a genuine interest in health data: the 5 Pro, with your expectations set that the AI side is the newest and least settled part of the package. Whichever you choose, buy during a bundle promotion, and use the 90-day trial as your real acceptance test, because your cat is the only one who can decide.
